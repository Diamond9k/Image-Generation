# Route, don't marry: generative media in 2026

As of October 2026, the right default for a new image or video product is **a thin, provider-agnostic job platform on top of hosted APIs**. Self-hosting comes later, and only for the narrow set of workloads where it pays. Leadership changes every two to four months, and a major lab can leave the market with six months' notice: OpenAI switched off the Sora 2 API on 24 September 2026 and named no replacement. In closed image generation, OpenAI's GPT Image 2.5 (Sunburst/Flare) holds #1 and #2 on both major arenas. Microsoft's MAI-Image, xAI's Grok Imagine 2.0, Google's Nano Banana 2, ByteDance's Seedream 5.0 Pro, Reve 2.1 and BFL's FLUX.2 make up a tightly bunched chase pack, at roughly $0.03–0.07 per image. In closed video the leaders are now mostly Chinese labs and Google's Gemini Omni Flash: Alibaba's Wan 3.0, ByteDance's Seedance 2.0/2.5, Kuaishou's Kling 3.0 and MiniMax H3. Native audio is standard, single-pass clips run up to 30 s, and good models cost $0.05–0.40 per output second. Open weights are close enough to matter for images but about 200 Elo behind for usable video. Licensing has also tightened: Wan stopped open-sourcing after 2.2, Qwen-Image-2.1 is research-only, and the strongest open video model (MiniMax H3) is geo-fenced out of the US, EU, UK and South Korea. The compliance floor rose in August 2026. EU AI Act Article 50 and California SB 942/AB 853 now require machine-readable marking of outputs, and the US TAKE IT DOWN Act requires 48-hour removal of non-consensual intimate imagery. Provenance (C2PA plus an invisible watermark), moderation and takedown are therefore launch requirements, not later additions. Read every figure below with one caveat: the research proxy blocked most primary sites (artificialanalysis.ai, vendor pricing pages, ai.google.dev, openai.com). Many prices and leaderboard numbers come from search snippets and aggregator blogs, and several sources conflict. Those conflicts are kept visible below.

## OpenAI tops image arenas, but ranks 4–11 sit within 100 Elo

In closed image generation, OpenAI regained a clear lead in 2026. **`gpt-image-2` launched on 21 April 2026** as OpenAI's first image model with native "thinking". It reasons about the image's structure before rendering, outputs at 2K, and can produce up to eight coherent images from one prompt ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/chatgpt-images-20-openai/)). It debuted at #1 on Artificial Analysis (AA) ([AA on X](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2047184062012706980?lang=en)). **GPT Image 2.5 followed on 8 September 2026** with up to 50% lower latency ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/08/openai-releases-chatgpt-images-2-5-with-sharper-details-and-more-precise-editing/)), split into two API models. **Flare** is the speed-optimised default, with 2–4x the throughput of GPT Image 2. **Sunburst** is the quality-max model ([DataNorth](https://datanorth.ai/news/openai-launches-chatgpt-images-2-5)). Both are billed per token ($5/M text input, $8/M image input, $30/M image output). Aggregators estimate a 1024² image at about **$0.006 (low) to $0.053 (high) and up to $0.21 at "Max"**, and 4K at Max at about $0.40 ([Atlas Cloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/gpt-image-2.5-api-cost)). Those figures were not checked against openai.com, and the "XHigh/Max" tier names come only from third parties. The binding constraint for a launch is rate limits rather than price. **Tier 1 allows only 5 images per minute and Tier 5 allows 250**, limits apply per organisation, and there is no free tier ([Atlas Cloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/gpt-image-2.5-rate-limits)).

The two arenas agree on the top of the table and disagree further down. AA's late-September snapshot of the new "v2.0" text-to-image board reads: **Sunburst 1196, Flare 1190, GPT Image 2 (high) 1171, Grok Imagine Image 2.0 1157, MAI-Image-2.6 1148** ([AA leaderboard snippet](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image)). LMArena's 24 September update (about 6.48M votes) reads: Sunburst 1424, Flare 1401, GPT Image 2 1383, MAI-Image-2.6 fourth. Google's best entry, **Nano Banana 2 in web-search mode, is only ninth (1261)**. Seedream 5.0 Pro and Qwen Image 3.0 Pro are tied at 1256 ([flami.pro summary](https://flami.pro/en/blog/lmarena-text-to-image-leaderboard)). Editing is a separate race. On AA's editing board **Microsoft's MAI-Image-2.6-Preview leads at 1286**. An earlier snapshot had MAI-Image-2.5-Pro (1272), Reve 2.1 (1263) and GPT Image 2 (1256) bunched together ([AA editing snippet](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/editing)). Because AA moved to v2.0 boards, Elo values from before and after the change cannot be compared. Ideogram 4.5, released 30 September, is probably not yet ranked.

Three structural shifts matter more than any single rank. First, **image generation has moved into multimodal LLMs**. Google deprecated Imagen 4 on 15 June and **shut it down in the Gemini API on 17 August 2026**, naming `gemini-3.1-flash-image` as the successor ([byteiota](https://byteiota.com/imagen-4-shutdown-august-17-migrate-to-gemini-image-api-now/); [Gemini deprecations](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deprecations)). The sources conflict here. A Google Cloud pricing page still lists Imagen 4 Fast/Standard/Ultra at $0.02/$0.04/$0.06 on Vertex ([Google Cloud pricing](https://cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/generative-ai/pricing)), so Vertex availability may differ from the Gemini API. One snippet also suggests the Nano Banana Pro *preview* endpoint was renamed or retired; that is unverified. The Nano Banana 2 release date conflicts too: AA reported it #1 in roughly late February 2026, while OpenRouter lists 18 June 2026, which is probably the GA date ([AA on X](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2027052241019175148); [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/google/gemini-3.1-flash-image)). Second, **every vendor now ships a cheap fast tier**, so the price of "good enough" has fallen to a few cents: Flare, Nano Banana 2 Lite at $0.034, MAI-Image-2.6-Flash, FLUX.2 Klein from $0.014, and Ideogram Turbo at $0.03. Third, **billing units do not match across vendors**. OpenAI, Google and Microsoft bill per token, BFL per megapixel, and xAI, ByteDance, Ideogram and Recraft per image. A product therefore has to normalise everything to cost per accepted image. Ranks 4–11 sit within about 40–100 Elo of each other, so mid-tier selection should be driven by price, latency, editing features and terms rather than rank.

## Video leadership moved East, and Sora's exit is the lock-in warning

Closed video changed more than any other segment in 2026. **OpenAI announced on 24 March 2026 that the Videos API and every Sora alias would be removed on 24 September 2026, and the deprecations page lists no replacement** ([Pondero](https://pondero.ai/news/2026-09-24-openai-sora-api-shutdown/); [OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/docs/deprecations)). The consumer app's shutdown date is reported inconsistently. Some sources say the app closed on 26 April 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sora_(text-to-video_model))). Engadget and WinBuzzer describe OpenAI shutting the app and web platform on 24 March, the date the $1B Disney licence collapsed with no money transferred ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/openai-is-shutting-down-its-sora-video-generation-app-211023358.html); [WinBuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/03/25/openai-kills-sora-video-app-billion-dollar-disney-deal-xcxwbn/)). The likeliest reading is announcement on 24 March and closure in late April, but this is unresolved. Several reseller listings still advertise Sora 2 (fal at $0.50/s, WaveSpeed at $0.10/s, Azure Foundry at $0.10/s), and those listings are probably stale. Azure availability after the shutdown is unconfirmed, and one Microsoft Q&A thread already reported Sora 2 missing from Foundry ([MS Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5774829/sora-2-not-available-in-azure-ai-foundry-model-list)).

The leaders that remain are mostly Chinese labs and Google. **Alibaba's Wan 3.0** (public beta 6 August, released 24 August 2026) is closed and API-only. It makes **up to 30 s in one pass** from text, images, audio, video or even PPT/PDF documents, at **$0.05/s (480p), $0.10/s (720p) and $0.20/s (1080p)** ([Alibaba Cloud](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/wan3-0-30-second-ai-video-generation-from-any-input_603452)). **ByteDance's Seedance 2.0** costs $0.04–0.78/s depending on variant and resolution, with audio included at no extra charge ([anikuku](https://anikuku.com/blog/seedance-2-api-pricing-guide-2026)). **Seedance 2.5** (released 31 July 2026) adds 30 s clips, up to 50 references and frame-local editing ([Kie.ai](https://kie.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-release-deep-dive), an unofficial source). One review prices it at about $0.23/s at 720p ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-review-pricing/)). **Kling 3.0** (6 February 2026) offers multilingual native audio, multi-shot storyboards and 4K, at a reported **$0.112/s (720p with audio) to $0.42/s (4K)** ([AtlasCloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/kling-3.0-review-features-pricing-ai-alternatives)). Kling raised consumer prices in April 2026 ([costbench](https://costbench.com/changelog/kling-price-increase-2026-04/)). Google split its line. **Gemini Omni Flash** (I/O, May 2026) is an any-input generator and conversational V2V editor with synchronized audio and 10 s clips, billed at roughly $0.10/s at 720p ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-gemini-omni-flash-video-model-io-2026); [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gemini-omni-1-1-flash-pricing)). **Veo 3.1** remains the developer-API line at $0.40/s Standard, $0.10–0.30/s Fast and $0.05–0.08/s Lite ([Puter](https://developer.puter.com/tutorials/gemini-api-pricing/)). Vertex lists Veo 3.1 Lite (no audio) from $0.03/s ([costgoat](https://costgoat.com/pricing/google-veo)), and sources disagree on whether Lite generates audio at all ([CometAPI](https://www.cometapi.com/what-is-google-veo-3-1-lite/); [Puter](https://developer.puter.com/ai/google/veo-3.1-lite/)).

In the West, **Runway Gen-4.5** costs $0.12/s and gained native audio in May 2026. Runway's V2V editor Aleph was replaced on 30 July 2026 by **Aleph 2.0 at $0.28/s** ([Runway changelog](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/api-details/api_changelog/); [apiframe](https://apiframe.ai/guides/runway-api-guide)). Luma's Ray3.14/3.2 is the HDR/EXR specialist at about $1.20 per 5 s 1080p SDR clip ([magichour](https://magichour.ai/blog/luma-dream-machine-pricing)). xAI's Grok Imagine video is the price leader at **$0.05–0.08/s**, with clips up to 15 s, or 30 s with extend ([apiframe](https://apiframe.ai/guides/grok-video-api-guide)). Whether its audio is native is disputed. BFL has also entered video: **FLUX 3 Video** appears on Runware at $0.17/s (720p) and $0.29/s (1080p), with a $0.04/s draft mode ([Runware](https://runware.ai/flux-3-video)). That lends some support to a "FLUX 3" entry on the AA video board, which the video researcher had suspected was an extraction error. For talking heads, specialist APIs are far cheaper than general video models. HeyGen Avatar IV runs about $3/min ([realtimeavatar.ai](https://realtimeavatar.ai/blog/heygen-api-pricing-explained)), and Tavus charges $1/generated minute and $0.37/real-time minute ([veed.io](https://www.veed.io/learn/best-talking-head-video-apis)).

## Open weights: near the frontier for images, behind for video, and licences are tightening

Open image weights are strong but more fragmented than a year ago. **FLUX.2 [dev]** (November 2025) is a 32B rectified-flow transformer paired with a 24B Mistral VLM encoder. It needs about 32 GB of VRAM in FP8, and GGUF quants are available for smaller cards ([Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/deploy-flux2-gpu-cloud-production-guide/)). **Z-Image-Turbo** (6B, Apache 2.0) runs in 8 steps, takes under a second on datacenter GPUs and fits on 16 GB consumer cards ([RunDiffusion](https://www.rundiffusion.com/z-image)). **HiDream-O1-Image** (8B, MIT, May 2026) is pixel-native, with no VAE and no separate text encoders, and supports output up to 2048² ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/HiDream-ai/HiDream-O1-Image)). **Qwen-Image-2512 and Edit-2511** (20.4B, Apache 2.0) remain the strongest openly licensed text renderers and editors ([QwenLM](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image)). At the large end, NVIDIA's **Cosmos3-Super-Text2Image** (65B, OpenMDW 1.1) and Tencent's HunyuanImage 3.0 (80B MoE) are datacenter-class ([creativeainews](https://www.creativeainews.com/articles/nvidia-cosmos-3-open-source-t2i-image-video-2026/); [layer3labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/comparisons/best-chinese-ai-models-for-image-generation)).

The open-versus-closed gap for images depends on which board you read, and this is a real conflict rather than noise. On AA's older text-to-image board, open models looked almost level with closed ones. Cosmos3-Super scored 1219–1230 and HiDream-O1-Dev-2604 about 1183–1187. A July aggregator snapshot even placed them above closed Ideogram 4.0 Quality (1164) ([techsy](https://techsy.io/en/blog/best-ai-image-models); [pinggy](https://pinggy.io/blog/best_free_open_source_ai_image_generators_to_self_host/)). On the new **v2.0 board, the best open entries are Qwen-Image-2.1 at 1035 and Ideogram 4.0 at 1010, against 1196 for GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst**, a gap of roughly 160 Elo ([AA snippet](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image)). Both of those "best open" models are also non-commercial. The earlier claim that open image models are "within 0–70 Elo" of the frontier should be treated as a pre-v2.0 artifact until a full v2.0 board can be read.

Video shows a clearer gap and a sharper licensing problem. **Alibaba stopped publishing Wan weights after 2.2.** Wan 2.5, 2.6, 2.7 and 3.0 are API-only, despite earlier promises and SEO sites that claim otherwise ([localaimaster](https://localaimaster.com/blog/wan-2-7-open-source); [AtlasCloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/is-wan-3.0-open-source)). **Lightricks' LTX-2.5** (about 11 August 2026) is the leading open model that Western businesses can actually use. It is a 22B dual-stream DiT that generates synchronized audio, outputs from 720p up to native 4K HDR and up to 50 fps, and makes 6–20 s clips ([OpenSourceForU](https://www.opensourceforu.com/2026/08/ltx-2-5-brings-open-weight-video-generation/)). On AA, however, **LTX-2.5 scores about 944–948 against the closed leader's 1157** ([AA open-weights T2V](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video/open-weights)). The model that nearly closes the gap, **MiniMax H3 (1138)**, ships under a licence that excludes the US, UK, EU and South Korea ([orcarouter](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/wan-3-0-vs-minimax-h3)). Sources disagree slightly on LTX-2's history: one says it was open-sourced on 6 January 2026 as 14B video plus 5B audio ([LTX blog](https://ltx.io/blog/ltx-2-is-now-open-source)), another that it was released in November 2025 with weights published later. **HunyuanVideo 1.5** (8.3B) runs in 14 GB of VRAM ([promptus](https://www.promptus.ai/blog/hunyuan-video-1-5)), but its licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea.

A team that wants self-hosted video with a clean commercial licence in the US or EU today is effectively choosing between Wan 2.2, which is behind on quality but has a rich LoRA and lip-sync ecosystem, and LTX-2.5, which is better but becomes a paid licence once revenue reaches $10M.

## APIs beat self-hosting until utilization and customization tip the math

For most teams, hosted media APIs win on cost as well as convenience. Commodity images are almost free. FLUX schnell costs **$0.0005 on DeepInfra** and $0.0006 on Runware, FLUX dev about $0.0038 on Runware, and FLUX.2-dev about $0.012 on fal ([DeepInfra](https://deepinfra.com/flux); [Puter](https://developer.puter.com/tutorials/flux-api-pricing/)). The same model can cost 5–10x more on one provider than on another. Closed video prices, by contrast, look like straight pass-throughs: **Veo 3/3.1 with audio is $0.40/s at Google, Replicate, Together and WaveSpeed alike** ([Replicate index](https://flexprice.io/pricing-index/replicate); [digitalapplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-image-generation-api-pricing-comparison-2026)). Aggregators therefore compete on open models and developer experience, not on closed-model margins. Fireworks and Cloudflare Workers AI serve images only. Gateways pass provider prices through: Vercel AI Gateway at zero markup with BYOK, OpenRouter with a 5.5% fee on credit purchases, and HF Inference Providers with no markup. Their value is routing and fallback, not price ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/i/vercel-ai-gateway-vs-openrouter); [HF docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/inference-providers/index)).

Self-hosting looks worse under real arithmetic. Serverless H100s cost about **$3.35–4.80/hr** (Modal $3.95, RunPod Active $3.35), and marketplace H100s cost **$1.49–2.27/hr** on Vast ([Spheron Modal](https://www.spheron.network/blog/modal-gpu-pricing-2026-per-second-billing/); [RunPod](https://www.runpod.io/articles/guides/nvidia-h100); [Spheron Vast](https://www.spheron.network/blog/vastai-pricing-2026/)). Unoptimized **Wan 2.2 14B takes about 10–12 minutes per 5 s 720p clip on an H100** ([Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/ai-video-generation-gpu-guide/)). That works out to about $0.145/video-second on Modal and $0.073/s on Vast, both higher than **fal's Wan 2.5 at $0.05/s** or Veo 3.1 Lite at $0.03–0.05/s, even at 100% utilization. FLUX dev at 10–20 s per image on a Modal H100 costs $0.011–0.022, against $0.0038 on Runware. The H100 throughput figure is a single snippet and depends heavily on steps and compilation. The break-even shifts only when three things hold together. The first is **high sustained utilization**: dedicated GPUs beat serverless above roughly 25–40%, which matches RunPod's ~25% breakpoint for Active workers. The second is **aggressive speedups**: 4-step distillation such as LightX2V, which cuts Wan-class models from 40–50 steps ([LightX2V](https://lightx2v-en.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_sources/method_tutorials/step_distill.md)), MagCache at 2.1–2.7x ([MagCache](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09045)), and FP8 or 4-bit quantization. The third is **workloads no API serves**, such as custom LoRAs, bespoke ComfyUI graphs or data-residency requirements. One fal figure of "$1.89/hr H100" is well below market and probably a committed-instance floor ([fal compute pricing](https://fal.ai/docs/documentation/compute/pricing)). No primary cold-start benchmarks were found for any provider.

On serving frameworks the evidence points to a clear division of labour. **ComfyUI is the day-0 runtime**: FLUX.2, HiDream-O1, HunyuanVideo 1.5, LTX-2.5 and MiniMax H3 all shipped with native or official workflows. Wrappers such as BentoML's comfy-pack, Baseten Truss and the Comfy Cloud API (now out of beta) turn those graphs into endpoints ([BentoML](https://www.bentoml.com/blog/comfy-pack-serving-comfyui-workflows-as-apis); [Comfy APIs](https://support.comfy.org/articles/1674221690-comfy-apis-an-overview)). At volume, though, ComfyUI's single-process design, missing native batching and WebSocket API become bottlenecks. One secondary source reports **2–3x more throughput from diffusers with torch.compile** behind a queue ([markaicode](https://markaicode.com/alternatives/comfyui-alternatives/)).

## Flow transformers, distillation and VLM judges define the 2026 toolchain

The technical recipe has converged. The 2026 default is a **rectified-flow transformer** (MMDiT or single-stream DiT) operating in a VAE latent space, which for video is a causal 3D VAE with spatiotemporal attention, conditioned by an LLM/VLM text encoder ([SD3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03206); [Wan report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20314)). Newer models break this template in two directions. Some are pixel-native (HiDream-O1, PRX Pixel). Others are unified multimodal transformers that also understand inputs, such as BAGEL, Emu3.5, Cosmos 3 and GPT Image's "thinking" ([BAGEL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14683); [Emu3.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26583)). Production speed comes from **few-step distillation**. LCM and ADD reach 1–4 steps for images, DMD/DMD2 match the teacher's output distribution, and for video, **Self Forcing and Causal Forcing (ICML 2026)** turn bidirectional teachers into causal, streaming students ([DMD2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14867); [Causal Forcing](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02214v2)). The cost is real. Distilled models usually run at CFG = 1, which gives up negative prompts and some diversity. The sensible pattern is to prototype on the full model and ship the distilled model with caching and quantization.

Customization has also simplified. **LoRA is still the universal unit**, and musubi-tuner is the broadest trainer. It was updated on 27 September 2026 and covers Wan, HunyuanVideo, FLUX.2, Qwen-Image, Z-Image, HiDream-O1, LTX and MiniMax H3 ([musubi-tuner](https://github.com/kohya-ss/musubi-tuner)). For many tasks, instruction- and reference-based editors (the Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit and FLUX.2 class) have replaced the IP-Adapter-plus-ControlNet stacks. The open video post-production stack is now well defined. **SeedVR2** (Apache 2.0, one-step) handles upscaling as a free Topaz alternative ([ComfyUI docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/utility/seedvr2)). RIFE and FILM handle interpolation. **LatentSync 1.6** handles short dubbing, and **InfiniteTalk** (Apache 2.0) handles unlimited-length talking video ([instavar](https://instavar.com/research/ai-video/open-source-lip-sync-models); [InfiniteTalk](https://github.com/MeiGen-AI/InfiniteTalk)). HunyuanVideo-Foley and MMAudio add sound, unless the generator already produces audio jointly. On the optimization side, **Nunchaku/SVDQuant 4-bit** claims 3.6x memory savings on FLUX, but its supported models do not include Wan video ([Nunchaku](https://github.com/nunchaku-ai/nunchaku)). SageAttention, MagCache and TeaCache cover video. diffusers now ships Modular Diffusers (stable as of v0.40), FlashAttention 4, and LTX-2.5/MiniMax-H3 pipelines ([diffusers releases](https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/releases)). Both A1111 and the original Forge repo are effectively legacy; Forge Neo is the maintained continuation.

Evaluation practice has moved past FID and CLIPScore. **GenEval is saturated**: a human study found Gemini 2.5 Flash Image scoring 96.7%, which prompted GenEval 2 and Soft-TIFA ([GenEval 2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398851070_GenEval_2_Addressing_Benchmark_Drift_in_Text-to-Image_Evaluation)). The working toolkit now has three parts. The first is VLM-based alignment, led by **VQAScore**, which significantly outperforms CLIPScore ([VQAScore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01291)). The second is learned preference models such as HPSv3 and ImageReward. The third is **VBench-2.0** for video, which scores human fidelity, physics, controllability and commonsense ([VBench-2.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21755v2)). Blind pairwise arenas serve as the final arbiter. Two pitfalls deserve attention. First, a 2026 paper shows that **guidance-scale choices skew T2I comparisons** ([Guidance Matters](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.22570)), so CFG and step counts must be held fixed when comparing models. Second, LLM prompt rewriters are now the default in first-party pipelines. Wan ships `prompt_extend.py`, and Qwen Image 3.0 rewrites prompts unless that is disabled ([Runware docs](https://runware.ai/docs/models/alibaba-qwen-image-3-0/guides/prompt-extension)). Rewriters can inflate scores, so evaluate with both the raw and the enhanced prompt.

## August 2026 turned provenance and takedown into launch requirements

Several regulatory deadlines converged in 2026. **EU AI Act Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026.** Generative providers must mark image, video, audio and text outputs in a machine-readable, detectable way, and deployers must disclose deepfakes. Systems already on the market before that date have until **2 December 2026** for the marking obligation ([EU Commission FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act)). The final Code of Practice expects **at least two techniques, signed metadata plus an imperceptible watermark** ([Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content)). The Paul Weiss page itself was blocked, and the late-2025 Digital Omnibus proposals may have shifted these dates; that could not be confirmed. **California SB 942, as amended by AB 853, became operative the same day.** Providers with more than 1M monthly users must offer a free detection tool and latent disclosures, with visible disclosure as an option ([Orrick](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2025-10-17/california-delays-its-ai-transparency-act-and-passes-new-content-laws/)). China has required visible and metadata labels since 1 September 2025 ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/chinas-ai-labeling-measures-and-mandatory-national-standards-take-effect-september-1)). In the US, the **FTC has enforced the TAKE IT DOWN Act since 19 May 2026**: platforms must remove non-consensual intimate imagery, including AI-generated imagery, and known copies within 48 hours, at up to $53,088 per violation ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-begins-enforcing-take-it-down-act)). The NO FAKES Act cleared Senate Judiciary on 22 June 2026 but is not yet law ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/4591)). In the UK, Ofcom's investigation of Grok's sexualized outputs under the Online Safety Act and the planned criminalisation of nudification tools show that regulators now treat capability as a target in itself, not just individual pieces of content ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2026/01/rd-ofcom-investigates-xs-grok-as-scrutiny-of-deepfakes-and-nudification-tools-increases)).

The major vendors have converged on provenance. **OpenAI adopted C2PA plus Google's SynthID in May 2026** and launched a "Verify" checker ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/19/openai-is-making-it-easier-to-check-if-an-image-was-made-by-their-models/)). Google watermarks Veo frame by frame ([Trust Insights](https://www.trustinsights.ai/blog/2026/04/ai-watermarks/)). Whether enterprises can switch SynthID off is undocumented, and the reasonable assumption is that they cannot. Watermark coverage for Kling, Seedance, Wan, Runway, Luma and xAI outputs was not verified. A product that routes across vendors therefore has to apply its own C2PA manifest and watermark at the post-processing step rather than relying on upstream marks. Upstream marks are also fragile: commercial "Veo watermark remover" services already exist ([example](https://www.gptcleanup.com/veo-video-watermark-remover)).

Copyright risk sits on the output side, and no court has yet ruled on training. **No US court has decided on the merits whether training image or video models is fair use.** The studios' cases against Midjourney and MiniMax have both survived dismissal and rest on characters generated on demand. The court found the MiniMax infringement claims plausible around May 2026 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/disneys-copyright-suit-against-chinese-ai-developer-advances)). In the Midjourney case, summary-judgment motions are reportedly due on 23 November 2026, with trial likely in 2027 ([ailawsuittracker](https://ailawsuittracker.com/blog/disney-v-midjourney-explained/)). **Getty v. Stability** survived dismissal in N.D. Cal. on 24 April 2026 ([Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/getty-images-us-inc-v-stability-ai-ltd)). In the UK, Getty is appealing the "model as infringing copy" point it lost at first instance ([IPKat](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2026/01/permission-to-appeal-granted-in-getty.html)). **Andersen's status conflicts across sources.** One blog said a jury trial would begin on 8 September 2026 ([Sigma Law](https://sigmalawgroup.com/blog/2026-09-06-andersen-stability-ai-jury-trial/)). A later docket-focused post (28 September) reports a three-month extension, with summary judgment not resolved before late 2027 ([ChatGPTiseatingtheworld](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/09/28/sarah-andersens-copyright-case-schedule-gets-extended-by-3-months)). The later source is the more credible of the two, and no verdict has been found. Andersen's surviving induced-infringement theory targets the *distribution* of weights, which matters for anyone redistributing open checkpoints. With **cert denied in *Thaler* on 2 March 2026**, raw AI outputs remain uncopyrightable without human authorship ([Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/march/supreme-court-denies-petition-on-copyright-authorship-by-ai)), so terms of service should not promise users copyright in what they generate. Indemnity exists but always comes with conditions. Adobe Firefly enterprise excludes customer inputs and modifications. OpenAI excludes claims involving disabled safety features or trademarks. Midjourney offers none ([Adobe FAQ](https://www.adobe.com/content/dam/dx/us/en/products/sensei/sensei-genai/firefly-enterprise/Firefly_Legal_FAQs_Enterprise_Customers.pdf); [Mike Chambers](https://www.mikechambers.com/blog/post/2025-09-24-generative-ai-commercial-safety/)). The vendor indemnity terms for 2026 were not verified for any provider.

## What a new team should actually build

The evidence supports a specific default architecture. **Treat every generation as an async job.** The API validates and moderates the prompt, optionally rewrites it with a cheap LLM, estimates cost from model, resolution, seconds and audio, and reserves credits atomically in Postgres. A router then submits the job to a provider queue with a signed webhook. fal's queue already provides webhooks, SSE progress, logs and retries ([fal queue docs](https://fal.ai/docs/documentation/model-apis/inference/queue)). Each model has a fallback list in priority order, every request carries an idempotency key, and a poller catches missed webhooks. On completion the system copies outputs immediately, because provider URLs expire. It then runs output moderation, which for video means sampling frames, applies its own C2PA manifest and watermark, and stores originals in **Cloudflare R2, which has zero egress at $0.015/GB-month** ([apiscout](https://apiscout.dev/guides/mux-vs-cloudflare-stream-api-2026)). Playback goes through **Cloudflare Stream ($1 per 1,000 minutes delivered) or Mux** ([Cloudflare Stream](https://f75b423c.previews.developers.cloudflare.com/stream/pricing/index.md)). Credits are captured on success and refunded on failure, using an append-only ledger that also records real provider cost per job. Delivery costs a fraction of a cent per clip, against $0.15–3.00 to generate it, so spend engineering effort on generation cost and routing, not storage. For starting code, Vercel's AI SDK image-generator template and its fal integration give a clean multi-provider base ([Vercel template](https://vercel.com/templates/next.js/ai-sdk-image-generator)). No mature open-source starter for a video-first product with timeline editing was found, so the job, webhook and timeline layers will be custom.

Model choices should follow from the use case and the licence, not from leaderboard position. Recommended defaults by use case:

| Need | Default (hosted) | Cheap/volume tier | Self-host option (clean licence) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero-quality images | GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst | Flare, Nano Banana 2 Lite ($0.034) | — |
| Mid-tier images at scale | Seedream 5.0 Pro, MAI-Image-2.6, FLUX.2 [pro] (~$0.03–0.05) | FLUX schnell/Klein ($0.0005–0.015) | Z-Image-Turbo, FLUX.2 klein-4B |
| Instruction editing | MAI-Image-2.6, GPT Image 2.5, Reve 2.1 | — | Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 |
| Typography / vector | Ideogram 4.5 / Recraft V4 SVG | Ideogram Turbo | HiDream-O1 (MIT) |
| IP-sensitive enterprise | Adobe Firefly (indemnified) | — | — |
| General video with audio | Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.x, Gemini Omni Flash | Grok Imagine, Wan 3.0 480p, Veo 3.1 Lite | LTX-2.5 (<$10M revenue), Wan 2.2 |
| Long single-pass (≤30 s) | Wan 3.0, Seedance 2.5 | — | — |
| Talking heads / avatars | HeyGen, Tavus | — | InfiniteTalk, LatentSync |

Two cautions go with this table. US and EU buyers should review data-residency and compliance terms for the Chinese video vendors that now lead on quality and price. And any model sourced only from aggregator pages (Seedance 2.5 specs, HappyHorse, Midjourney's API) needs a direct vendor check before it carries production traffic. On evaluation, build an internal prompt set before choosing a model. Score candidates with VQAScore or a VLM judge, HPSv3/ImageReward and VBench-2.0 dimensions at fixed CFG and steps, and run blind A/B tests, because arena ranks for the same model disagree by board, version and audio setting. On safety, launch with prompt, input and output classification, CSAM hash-matching with NCMEC reporting, filters for protected characters and logos, consent verification for uploaded faces in image-to-video, and a 48-hour takedown workflow. Bring self-hosting in last. It earns its place only for a workload with steady utilization above roughly 25–40%, distilled and quantized checkpoints, or custom LoRAs. Use ComfyUI to explore and port the hot paths to diffusers behind a queue on Modal or RunPod.

## Conclusion

The main lesson of 2026 is that **the scarce asset is no longer access to a good model but insulation from model churn**. Within nine months, the top image slot changed hands four times, Imagen was retired, Sora was shut down, Wan was closed, Qwen-Image's licence was downgraded and Kling's prices went up. A product that hard-codes a provider, a licence or an upstream watermark will need rework within a year. A product built around a router, its own provenance layer and its own evaluation harness can absorb those changes and switch to whichever model is cheapest for the quality it needs. The open-weight bet has also changed character. In 2025 open weights served as a hedge against closed vendors. In 2026 they are a licensing minefield whose best video options are either revenue-gated (LTX) or geo-fenced (H3, Hunyuan). For most teams, open weights are now the specialist tool for customization and residency rather than the default cost saver.

Several assumptions should be rechecked often, because the evidence base is thinner than the polish of vendor pages suggests. Many prices in this report come from resellers, the leaderboards conflict across versions, and the dates for Imagen's Vertex status, Sora's shutdown, Veo Lite's audio and the Andersen trial are all disputed. The legal position on training has not been settled by any court and could turn on the November 2026 summary-judgment filings in the Midjourney case. The compliance deadlines, by contrast, are already in force. That asymmetry favours spending early engineering effort on provenance, moderation and takedown, which are certain obligations, rather than on model selection, which will need revisiting regardless.
